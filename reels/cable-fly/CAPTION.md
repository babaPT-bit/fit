# Instagram caption — Cable Fly: 3 angles

Same exercise. Three cable heights. Three different jobs for your chest. 🧵

🔻 HIGH → LOW (pulley above, hands finish at your hips)
The cable pulls your arm up and out, so your chest has to pull it *down and in*.
That's the line of the lower (costal) fibres → **lower chest ~60% of the work**.

➖ MID (pulley at shoulder height, hands meet in front of your chest)
A pure "hug" — horizontal adduction. The middle (sternal) fibres line up best
→ **mid chest ~50%**, upper chest helping.

🔺 LOW → HIGH (pulley at the floor, hands finish at chin height)
Now your arm has to move *up and in*. That's the job of the upper (clavicular)
fibres → **upper chest ~55%**, with the front delts doing a big share (~40%).

⚡ Bonus: on all three, tension peaks about ⅓ of the way into the rep —
when the cable is closest to 90° to your arm — and drops at the squeeze.
Control the stretch; don't just chase the squeeze.

How we got these numbers: a simple model of the shoulder. Leverage = the
moment arm of the cable about the shoulder joint; the split between chest
regions = how well each fibre group's pulling direction matches the torque
needed. It's a model estimate, not an EMG study — real numbers vary with your
stance, arm length and how far you step forward.
