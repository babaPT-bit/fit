# "Same move. Different muscle." – doodle series scripts

Style for every video: rugged paper, black-ink stickman with eyes and a smile, the working
muscle pops in red marker, then a magnifier bubble zooms into a hand-drawn muscle split into
its parts. The working part is red-hatched with a red label; the other parts are grey pencil.
Same voice and handwritten captions as the biceps test. ~30 s each, about 75–85 spoken words.

Every video follows the same 5 beats:

| Time | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | **Hook**: the confusion | Both stickmen side by side, a "?" scribble above them |
| 3–12 s | **Version A** | Stickman A does 2 slow reps, muscle pops, zoom bubble |
| 12–21 s | **Version B** | Stickman B does 2 slow reps, muscle pops, zoom bubble |
| 21–27 s | **Why**: the one-line reason | Both bubbles side by side, the key difference circled |
| 27–30 s | **Takeaway + CTA** | Both stickmen give a thumbs up, "Save this" scribble |

---

## 1. Preacher curl vs behind-the-body cable curl (biceps heads)
**Title:** Same curl. Two heads.

- **Hook:** "Same curl, but your elbow position decides which biceps head grows."
- **A, preacher curl (arm in front):** "Arm in front, on the pad. The long head goes slack, so the short head does the heavy lifting."
- **B, cable curl facing away (arm behind):** "Arm behind your body. Now the long head is stretched, and it takes over."
- **Why:** "The long head crosses your shoulder. Move the arm, and you change its length."
- **Takeaway:** "Do one in front, one behind, and you've trained the whole biceps. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** biceps split into short head (inner) and long head (outer); tendons up to the shoulder.

## 2. Regular curl vs hammer curl
**Title:** Palms up or thumbs up?

- **Hook:** "Regular curl or hammer curl? They don't hit the same muscle."
- **A, palms up:** "Palms up, and the biceps works hardest, because it also turns your palm up."
- **B, thumbs up (hammer):** "Thumbs up, and the work shifts to the brachialis under your biceps, and the brachioradialis in your forearm."
- **Why:** "A thicker brachialis pushes your biceps up, so your arm looks bigger."
- **Takeaway:** "Big arms need both grips. Save this for arm day."
- **Zoom bubble:** upper arm with biceps on top, brachialis underneath, brachioradialis running into the forearm.

## 3. Overhead triceps extension vs pushdown
**Title:** Where's the biggest part of your triceps?

- **Hook:** "Two-thirds of your arm is triceps, and most people only do pushdowns."
- **A, pushdown (arms at your sides):** "Pushdowns hit the outer and middle heads hard, but the long head stays short and easy."
- **B, overhead extension (arms up):** "Arms overhead stretch the long head, the biggest of the three."
- **Why:** "One study found about forty percent more triceps growth with overhead extensions than pushdowns."
- **Takeaway:** "Keep the pushdowns, but add one overhead move. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** back of the arm with three heads: long (inner, up to the shoulder blade), lateral (outer), medial (deep, near the elbow).

## 4. Incline vs flat vs decline bench press
**Title:** Which bench angle builds which chest?

- **Hook:** "Flat, incline or decline? Here's what each one really trains."
- **A, flat bench:** "Flat bench hits the big middle part of your chest."
- **B, incline at about thirty degrees:** "Tilt the bench to about thirty degrees and the upper chest, under your collarbone, works more."
- **Short C, decline:** "Decline leans toward the lower fibres, which flat bench already hits well."
- **Why:** "The angle changes which fibres line up with the push."
- **Takeaway:** "Most people only need flat and incline. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** chest fan with upper (clavicular), middle (sternal) and lower (costal) fibres; the matching band turns red for each angle.
- Note: three setups, so the B and C beats run about 6 s each.

## 5. Front raise vs lateral raise vs rear delt fly
**Title:** Your shoulder has three parts.

- **Hook:** "Your shoulder isn't one muscle. It's three."
- **A, front raise:** "Front raise: front delt. But presses already hammer this one."
- **B, lateral raise:** "Lateral raise, arms out to the side: side delt, the one that makes shoulders look wide."
- **C, rear delt fly:** "Bent over, arms out: rear delt, the one almost everyone skips."
- **Why:** "Each part lifts your arm in a different direction."
- **Takeaway:** "For round shoulders, prioritise side and rear. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** shoulder cap split into front, side and rear delt; each turns red in turn.

## 6. Squat vs Romanian deadlift
**Title:** Knees or hips?

- **Hook:** "Squats and RDLs both work your legs, but not the same part."
- **A, squat:** "In a squat your knees bend a lot, so your quads on the front of your thigh do most of the work, with your glutes."
- **B, Romanian deadlift:** "In an RDL your knees stay soft and your hips push back. That stretches your hamstrings and glutes."
- **Why:** "Bend at the knee: quads. Hinge at the hip: hamstrings."
- **Takeaway:** "One of each, every leg day. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** thigh, front and back: quads (front) vs hamstrings (back), glutes on top.

## 7. Seated vs lying leg curl
**Title:** This leg curl grows more hamstring.

- **Hook:** "Seated or lying leg curl? One grew noticeably more hamstring in a study."
- **A, lying leg curl:** "Lying down, your hips are straight, so your hamstrings start the curl already short."
- **B, seated leg curl:** "Sitting, your hips are bent, so your hamstrings are stretched from both ends."
- **Why:** "Training a muscle while it's long seems to grow it more. Seated won in a head-to-head study."
- **Takeaway:** "If your gym has both, pick seated. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** hamstring drawn as a rubber band from hip to knee. In the lying view it's short and slack; in the seated view it's long and taut, with a red stretch arrow.

## 8. Standing vs seated calf raise
**Title:** Straight knees or bent knees?

- **Hook:** "Your calf is two muscles, and your knees decide which one works."
- **A, standing calf raise:** "Knees straight: the gastrocnemius, the diamond-shaped muscle you can see, does the work."
- **B, seated calf raise:** "Knees bent: the gastrocnemius goes slack, and the soleus underneath takes over."
- **Why:** "The gastrocnemius crosses your knee. The soleus doesn't."
- **Takeaway:** "Do both, and go slow at the bottom. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** lower leg with the gastrocnemius (two bellies, top) over the soleus (underneath, wider at the bottom).

## 9. Lat pulldown vs straight-arm pulldown
**Title:** Can't feel your lats?

- **Hook:** "Doing pulldowns but only feeling your arms?"
- **A, lat pulldown:** "Regular pulldowns bend your elbows, so your biceps help a lot, along with your lats."
- **B, straight-arm pulldown:** "Keep your arms straight and sweep the bar to your hips. Now your biceps can't help, so your lats do all the work."
- **Why:** "No elbow bend means no biceps."
- **Takeaway:** "Use straight-arm first to wake up your lats, then pulldowns. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** back view with lats (big wings) red and biceps grey. For the regular pulldown, both are lightly red.

## 10. Crunch vs hanging leg raise
**Title:** Upper abs vs lower abs?

- **Hook:** "Upper abs and lower abs? Here's the truth."
- **A, crunch:** "A crunch curls your ribs toward your hips. That's your six-pack muscle, the rectus abdominis."
- **B, hanging leg raise:** "Lifting your legs is mostly your hip flexors. Your abs only kick in when you curl your hips up at the top."
- **Why:** "Your six-pack is one long muscle. You can't split upper from lower, but you can do the move right."
- **Takeaway:** "On leg raises, curl your pelvis up, don't just swing your legs. Save this."
- **Zoom bubble:** torso with the rectus abdominis (one long band) vs the hip flexor running from spine to thigh. In the leg raise, the hip flexor is red; in the curl-up at the top, the abs turn red.

### Accuracy notes
- Every pair shifts **emphasis**; the other muscles still work. The voiceover says "more" or "takes over", never "only", except for the straight-arm pulldown (no elbow bend means the biceps can't help) and the crunch/leg-raise explanation.
- #3 and #7 cite single studies (Maeo et al.: overhead vs pushdown triceps, seated vs lying leg curl). The voiceover says "one study" so it doesn't overclaim.
